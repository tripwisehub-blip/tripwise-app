Flight delayed or cancelled? Your rights to compensation in Europe

EU Regulation 261/2004 explained simply: when you can claim up to €600 for a delayed or cancelled flight.

Under EU Regulation 261/2004, passengers can receive between €250 and €600 per person when a flight arrives more than three hours late, is cancelled at short notice or is overbooked. Many travellers never claim.

Which flights are covered?

  • All flights departing from an airport in the EU (plus Iceland, Norway and Switzerland), with any airline.
  • Flights arriving in the EU when operated by an EU-based airline.
  • The UK has an equivalent rule (UK261) for flights to and from the UK.

How much?

  • €250 for flights up to 1,500 km.
  • €400 for flights within the EU over 1,500 km, and other flights of 1,500–3,500 km.
  • €600 for flights over 3,500 km (may be reduced by half if the delay is 3–4 hours).

When airlines don't have to pay

Compensation is not due for "extraordinary circumstances" such as severe weather, air traffic control restrictions, security threats or some strikes not involving airline staff. Technical problems usually do not count as extraordinary.

Your other rights

Regardless of compensation, after a long delay the airline must provide meals and refreshments, and hotel accommodation if you are stuck overnight. Keep your receipts.

How to claim

  1. Keep your boarding pass and booking confirmation; note the actual arrival time.
  2. Submit a claim directly to the airline, or
  3. use a claims service that handles the process for a share of the compensation, only if successful – for example AirHelp.

Claims can often be made for flights several years in the past, depending on the country.
