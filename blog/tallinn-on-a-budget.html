Tallinn on a budget: a practical 3-day guide

Where to stay, what to see and how to save money in Tallinn, Estonia's medieval capital.

Updated October 2026 · Prices and schedules change – always check current details before booking.

Tallinn packs a UNESCO-listed Old Town, a creative harbour district and a wild Baltic coastline into a city you can cross by tram in twenty minutes. It is also one of the easier Nordic-Baltic capitals to visit on a modest budget, if you plan a little.

When to go

June to August brings long white nights and open-air festivals, but also cruise-ship crowds and the highest hotel prices. May and September are the sweet spot: mild weather, fewer people, lower rates. December is magical thanks to the Christmas market on Town Hall Square, though days are short and cold.

Where to stay

  • Old Town – unbeatable atmosphere, but the most expensive and noisy at weekends.
  • Kesklinn (city centre) and Rotermann – modern hotels a five-minute walk from the Old Town, often 20–30% cheaper.
  • Kalamaja and Telliskivi – wooden houses, cafés and the Telliskivi Creative City; good value apartments and a local feel.
  • Kadriorg – quiet, green, next to the park and the art museum; ideal for families.

Getting around

Tallinn Airport is only 4 km from the centre and tram line 4 runs straight into town, so you rarely need a taxi. Public transport tickets are bought with a contactless bank card on board. If you arrive late at night with luggage or children, a pre-booked airport transfer saves stress. For day trips to Lahemaa National Park or the beaches of Pärnu, renting a car is the most flexible option.

What to see

  • Toompea hill viewpoints (Kohtuotsa and Patkuli) – free and best at sunset.
  • St Olaf's Church tower and the Town Hall tower for views over the red roofs.
  • Kadriorg Palace and park, and the KUMU art museum.
  • Seaplane Harbour (Lennusadam) – a submarine, icebreaker and seaplanes under one roof.
  • Telliskivi Creative City and the Balti Jaama market for street food and vintage shops.

Museum and attraction tickets can be booked online in advance, which helps on busy summer days. Prefer exploring at your own pace? A self-guided audio tour of the Old Town costs less than a group tour.

Money-saving tips

  • Eat your main meal at lunch: many restaurants offer a "päevapraad" (daily lunch special) at a fraction of the dinner price.
  • Tap water is excellent – carry a bottle.
  • Walk: the Old Town, Kalamaja and the harbour are all within 25 minutes on foot.
  • A day trip to Helsinki by ferry takes about two hours each way – watch for midweek ferry deals.

Staying connected

Estonia is famously digital and free Wi-Fi is everywhere, but if you are coming from outside the EU, an eSIM data plan is usually far cheaper than roaming.
