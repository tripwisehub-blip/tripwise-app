eSIM for travel: how it works and how much you can save

A simple explanation of travel eSIMs, which phones support them and how they compare with roaming.

An eSIM is a digital SIM card built into your phone. Instead of buying a plastic SIM at the airport, you install a data plan by scanning a QR code or tapping a link – often before you leave home.

Why use a travel eSIM?

  • Cost – roaming outside your home region can be very expensive; local or regional eSIM data packs are usually much cheaper.
  • Convenience – you're online the moment you land, for maps, taxis and messages.
  • Keep your number – your normal SIM stays active for calls and SMS (e.g. bank codes), while the eSIM handles data.

Does my phone support eSIM?

Most iPhones from the XS/XR onwards, recent Google Pixel phones and many Samsung Galaxy S, Z and newer A models support eSIM. The phone must also be unlocked from your carrier. On iPhone, look for "Add eSIM" in Settings → Mobile Data; on Android, check Settings → Connections → SIM manager.

How to set it up

  1. Choose a plan for your destination country or region (e.g. Europe, Asia, global).
  2. Install the eSIM while you still have Wi-Fi – ideally the day before departure.
  3. On arrival, switch mobile data to the eSIM and turn on data roaming for that line.

Tips

  • Regional plans are good value if you visit several countries.
  • Most travel eSIMs are data-only; use WhatsApp or Telegram for calls.
  • Don't delete the eSIM until you've used up or no longer need the plan.
