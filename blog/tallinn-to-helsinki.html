Tallinn to Helsinki: ferry, flight or day trip – how to choose

Everything you need to cross the Gulf of Finland: ferry companies, travel times, tickets, and how to do Helsinki as a day trip from Tallinn.

Tallinn and Helsinki are only about 80 km apart, and the ferry between them is one of the busiest sea routes in Europe. Millions of people cross every year for work, shopping and short city breaks. Here is how to choose the right option.

Ferry: the default choice

Three companies run the route all year: Tallink (fast ships such as Megastar and MyStar, about 2 hours), Viking Line (about 2.5 hours) and Eckerö Line (often the cheapest, about 2–2.5 hours). There are many sailings a day in each direction.

  • Book online in advance – fares change with demand, and Friday evenings, Sunday afternoons and holidays are the most expensive.
  • Foot passengers can usually check in by scanning the ticket at the gate; arrive 30–45 minutes before departure.
  • Cars need to arrive earlier and cost more – useful if you continue to Lapland or around Finland.
  • In Tallinn, most ships leave from the Old City Harbour (terminals A and D), a 15–20 minute walk from the Old Town. In Helsinki, check whether your ship arrives at West Harbour (Länsisatama) or the South/Katajanokka terminals.

Flying

There is a short flight between the two cities, but once you add airport transfers and security, it is rarely faster than the ferry. It can make sense when you connect to a long-haul flight from Helsinki – compare flight prices with the ferry before deciding.

Helsinki as a day trip from Tallinn

A day trip works well: take a morning ferry, spend 6–8 hours in the city and return in the evening. A simple route:

  1. Walk from the harbour to the Market Square and the Old Market Hall.
  2. See Senate Square and the Helsinki Cathedral, then the Uspenski Cathedral.
  3. Take the public ferry (part of the HSL network) to Suomenlinna sea fortress.
  4. Finish with the Esplanadi park, the Oodi library and the Temppeliaukio rock church.

Museum tickets and boat tours are easy to book online so you don't waste time in queues.

Day trip to Tallinn from Helsinki

Many Finns do the reverse trip. Tallinn's medieval Old Town starts a short walk from the harbour, so you can see the Town Hall Square, Toompea hill and the viewing platforms in a few hours. Read our Tallinn budget guide for details.

Tips

  • Bring your passport or EU ID card – both countries are in Schengen, but ferry companies can ask for ID.
  • Your phone works in both countries with an EU SIM; visitors from outside the EU can use an eSIM.
  • If your ferry arrives late at night, a pre-booked transfer is easier than looking for a taxi.
