Lithuania

Vilnius in a weekend: baroque streets, Užupis and great value

A compact guide to Lithuania's capital: what to see, where to stay and how to keep costs low.

Updated October 2026 · Prices and schedules change – always check current details before booking.

Vilnius has one of the largest surviving medieval old towns in Northern and Eastern Europe, and it is UNESCO-listed. It is full of baroque churches, courtyards and cafés, and prices are usually lower than in the Nordic capitals.

What to see

  • Old Town – Cathedral Square, Pilies Street, the Gate of Dawn and St Anne's Church, a gothic gem.
  • Gediminas Tower – climb or take the funicular for a view over the red roofs.
  • Užupis – the self-declared "republic" of artists, with its constitution displayed on a wall in many languages.
  • Trakai – an island castle 30 minutes away by bus or train, perfect for a half-day trip.

Where to stay

Stay in or next to the Old Town to walk everywhere. Naujamiestis, just west of it, has newer hotels and apartments at lower prices.

Getting around

Vilnius Airport is close to the centre, about 15 minutes by bus or train. For late arrivals, a pre-booked transfer is simple. To see Trakai, the Hill of Crosses or the Curonian Spit, rent a car: Lithuanian roads are good and distances short.

Budget tips

  • Try cepelinai (potato dumplings) and šaltibarščiai (cold pink beet soup) in local canteens for a cheap, filling meal.
  • Many churches are free to visit.
  • Use public transport apps or contactless payment instead of taxis.
