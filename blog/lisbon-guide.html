Lisbon for first-timers: neighbourhoods, trams and day trips

Where to stay in Lisbon, how to get around the hills, and the best day trips to Sintra and Cascais.

Updated October 2026 · Prices and schedules change – always check current details before booking.

Lisbon spreads over seven hills above the Tagus river. Sunny viewpoints, tiled façades and pastéis de nata make it one of Europe's favourite city breaks.

Where to stay

  • Baixa and Chiado – central and flat between the hills, close to everything.
  • Alfama – the oldest district, full of character and fado bars, but steep.
  • Príncipe Real – elegant, quieter, with gardens and boutiques.
  • Cais do Sodré – lively at night and next to the train to Cascais.

Getting around

Comfortable shoes are essential. Trams, funiculars and the metro make the hills easier; a rechargeable transport card is cheaper than single tickets. The metro runs from the airport to the centre in about 25 minutes. A pre-booked transfer helps with luggage.

Must-sees

  • Belém: Jerónimos Monastery and the tower by the river.
  • Miradouros (viewpoints) like Senhora do Monte and Santa Catarina – free and best at sunset.
  • LX Factory and the Time Out Market for food.

Day trips

Sintra's palaces are 40 minutes away by train; go early on a weekday and book palace tickets in advance. Cascais has beaches reachable by train along the coast. To explore the Algarve or the Alentejo, rent a car.
