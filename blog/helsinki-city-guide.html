Helsinki in 2–3 days: design, saunas and islands

A practical Helsinki guide: where to stay, sauna culture, Suomenlinna and how to save money in Finland.

Updated October 2026 · Prices and schedules change – always check current details before booking.

Helsinki is relaxed, walkable and surrounded by sea. Finland has a reputation for being expensive, but with the right choices a few days here can be surprisingly affordable.

Top experiences

  • Suomenlinna – a UNESCO sea fortress on islands, reached by the regular city ferry from Market Square (included in a public transport ticket).
  • Public saunas – Löyly, Allas Sea Pool and the traditional Kotiharjun sauna. Swimming in the sea afterwards is part of the experience, even in winter.
  • Design District – boutiques, galleries and the Design Museum south of Esplanadi.
  • Temppeliaukio rock church and the Oodi central library, a striking modern building that is free to enter.
  • Old Market Hall for salmon soup and Finnish pastries.

Where to stay

  • Kamppi and Kluuvi – central, close to the bus and train stations.
  • Punavuori – the design district, with cafés and restaurants.
  • Kallio – the former working-class district, now lively and usually cheaper.

Transport

The train from Helsinki-Vantaa Airport to the centre takes about 30 minutes; buy an HSL ticket for zones ABC. A day ticket covers trams, metro, buses and the Suomenlinna ferry. Travelling with lots of luggage? Book an airport transfer in advance. Nuuksio National Park and the old town of Porvoo make great day trips – a car rental makes them easier.

Save money in Helsinki

  • Lunch buffets ("lounas") on weekdays are the best-value meal in Finland.
  • Supermarkets like Lidl and K-Market sell good ready-made food.
  • Many museums have free entry on certain evenings – check before you go.
  • Combine with Tallinn: ferries cross the Gulf of Finland many times a day.

Book museum tickets and tours online, and get an eSIM if you're visiting from outside the EU.
