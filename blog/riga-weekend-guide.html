A weekend in Riga: Art Nouveau, markets and good value

How to spend 48 hours in Riga, Latvia: neighbourhoods, sights, food and transport tips.

Updated October 2026 · Prices and schedules change – always check current details before booking.

Riga is the largest city in the Baltics and one of Europe's most underrated weekend breaks. It combines a compact medieval centre with the world's finest collection of Art Nouveau buildings and a huge food market housed in former Zeppelin hangars.

Day 1: Old Riga and the market

Start at the House of the Blackheads and Town Hall Square, then wander to St Peter's Church – its tower lift gives the best panorama of the city. Continue past the Three Brothers and the Swedish Gate. In the afternoon, head to the Central Market: five pavilions of smoked fish, cheese, rye bread and seasonal berries. It is the cheapest place in town for a picnic lunch.

Day 2: Art Nouveau and the parks

Alberta and Elizabetes streets are lined with ornate façades by Mikhail Eisenstein and others. The Art Nouveau Museum shows a restored apartment from 1903. Afterwards, walk through Bastejkalns park along the canal to the Freedom Monument. In the evening, try a Latvian beer hall or the bars of the Miera iela district.

Where to stay

  • Old Riga (Vecrīga) – walk everywhere, but check that your room isn't above a bar.
  • Centrs / Quiet Centre – the Art Nouveau quarter; elegant and quieter, often better value.
  • Ķīpsala – across the river with skyline views; good for families and drivers.

Getting there and around

Riga Airport (RIX) is the main Baltic hub, with low-cost routes across Europe – compare flight prices across airlines and dates, as midweek departures are often cheaper. Bus 22 links the airport and the centre in about 30 minutes; a pre-booked transfer is handy for late arrivals. Jūrmala's beaches are 30 minutes away by train, and a rental car opens up Gauja National Park and Sigulda castle.

Budget tips

  • Business-lunch menus on weekdays offer two or three courses at low prices.
  • Many churches and the Art Nouveau streets are free to explore.
  • Buy public transport tickets in advance rather than from the driver.
