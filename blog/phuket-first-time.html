Phuket for first-timers: best beaches, 6-day plan and practical tips

First trip to Phuket? Which beach to stay on (Patong, Kata, Kamala, Old Town), best time to go, a simple 6-day plan with Phi Phi and Phang Nga, getting around and cheap flights.

Phuket is Thailand's largest island and the easiest beach escape for a first trip to Southeast Asia: long sandy bays, island-hopping by boat, night markets and plenty of hotels at every price. The high season runs through the European winter, which makes it one of the most popular warm getaways from November to April.

When to go

  • November–April is the dry, sunny high season with calm seas on the west coast — the best time for beaches and boat trips. Christmas, New Year and February are the busiest and most expensive.
  • May–October is the monsoon season: more rain and bigger waves on the west coast, but lower hotel prices and fewer crowds. Some boat trips may be cancelled in rough weather.

Which beach to stay on

  • Patong — the busiest and liveliest, with nightlife, shopping and many budget hotels. Great if you want energy; not for a quiet holiday.
  • Kata and Karon — wide sandy beaches, a calmer atmosphere and good for families, still close to restaurants.
  • Kamala and Surin — quieter and a little more upscale, with beach clubs and sunset views.
  • Bang Tao — a long beach with large resorts, good for a relaxed stay.
  • Phuket Old Town — colourful Sino-Portuguese streets, cafés and the Sunday walking market; not on the beach, but cheap and full of character.

A simple 6-day plan

  1. Day 1: arrive at Phuket airport (HKT), transfer to your beach and relax.
  2. Day 2: beach day, then sunset at Promthep Cape.
  3. Day 3: boat trip to the Phi Phi Islands and Maya Bay.
  4. Day 4: Phang Nga Bay — limestone cliffs, sea caves and "James Bond Island".
  5. Day 5: Big Buddha, Wat Chalong and Phuket Old Town; evening at a night market.
  6. Day 6: snorkelling at Racha Island or a lazy last beach day.

Island-hopping trips, speedboat tours and cooking classes are easy to book in advance on KKday or GetYourGuide — worth doing in high season, when the best boats fill up.

Getting around

  • Phuket is bigger than it looks: the airport is in the north, the main beaches are 40–60 minutes away by car.
  • Book an airport transfer in advance so a driver waits for you at arrivals.
  • Ride-hailing apps work across the island and are usually cheaper than street taxis and tuk-tuks.
  • For your own wheels, compare rental cars; only ride a scooter if you are experienced and have the right licence and insurance.

Practical tips

  • The currency is the Thai baht (THB). Cards work in hotels and malls; carry cash for markets, street food and small shops.
  • Check the current entry rules for your nationality before you fly.
  • Get an eSIM before departure so maps and ride apps work right after landing.
  • Respect red flags on the beach — rip currents are common, especially outside the dry season.
  • Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples.

Cheap flights to Phuket

From Europe, Phuket is usually reached with one stop via Bangkok, Istanbul, Doha, Dubai or Singapore, and there are seasonal direct flights from some cities in winter.
