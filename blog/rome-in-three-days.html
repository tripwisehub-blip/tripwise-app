Rome in three days: an efficient itinerary

See the Colosseum, Vatican and Trastevere in three days, with tips on tickets, transport and where to stay.

Rome rewards walkers: almost all the great sights sit within a few kilometres of each other. Three days is enough for the highlights if you book the big-ticket sites ahead.

Day 1: Ancient Rome

Start early at the Colosseum, then continue to the Roman Forum and Palatine Hill (one combined ticket, timed entry). In the afternoon, walk to Piazza Venezia, the Capitoline Hill and the Pantheon. End with dinner in the Monti district.

Day 2: Vatican and Prati

The Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel are among the busiest attractions in the world – pre-booked skip-the-line tickets save hours. St Peter's Basilica is free but has a security queue; go early or late. Have lunch in the Prati district, then cross the river to Castel Sant'Angelo.

Day 3: Piazzas and Trastevere

Trevi Fountain at 8 am, the Spanish Steps, Piazza Navona and Campo de' Fiori. Spend the evening in Trastevere for trattorias and street life. If you prefer to explore independently, a self-guided audio tour adds context without a group.

Where to stay

  • Centro Storico – walk to everything, higher prices.
  • Monti – near the Colosseum, charming and central.
  • Prati – near the Vatican, calmer and good value.
  • Trastevere – lively evenings, cobbled lanes.

Practical tips

  • The Leonardo Express train links Fiumicino airport and Termini station; a private transfer is often similar in price for three or four people.
  • Drink from the free "nasoni" water fountains around the city.
  • Cover shoulders and knees for churches.
  • Many state museums are free on the first Sunday of the month – expect crowds.
