Budapest on a budget: baths, views and ruin bars

How to enjoy Budapest without overspending: where to stay, the thermal baths, free viewpoints, transport passes and food tips.

Updated October 2026. Prices and schedules change, so check current details before booking.

Budapest is two cities in one: hilly Buda with the castle on one bank of the Danube and lively Pest with grand boulevards on the other. It is still one of the better-value capitals in Central Europe, especially if you follow a few simple rules.

Where to stay

  • District V (Belváros) – central, walkable, near the river and the Parliament.
  • District VII (Jewish Quarter) – ruin bars, cafés and nightlife; can be noisy.
  • District VIII and IX – cheaper and well connected by metro and tram.

Getting around

Buy a 24-hour, 72-hour or weekly travel card instead of single tickets; it covers metro, trams and buses, including tram 2 along the Danube, one of the most scenic rides in Europe. The 100E airport bus runs to the centre; a pre-booked transfer is easier late at night.

What to see for free or cheap

  • Walk up to Fisherman's Bastion and the Castle District; the lower terraces are free.
  • Gellért Hill for the best panoramic view of the city.
  • Margaret Island, the Central Market Hall and the Shoes on the Danube Bank memorial.

Thermal baths

Széchenyi and Gellért are the famous ones, while smaller baths are often cheaper and less crowded. Weekday mornings are the quietest. Bath entry and Danube cruises can be booked online to skip the queues.

Food and money tips

  • Pay in forints and choose to be charged in local currency on card terminals.
  • Look for lunch menus (napi menü) on weekdays – a soup and main for much less than dinner prices.
  • Check that a service charge is not already on the bill before tipping.
